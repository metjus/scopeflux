What do these numbers mean?
Latest value
The most recent observation, in the original data unit. Wikipedia views count article reads, not searches; an interest index is relative, not a count of people.
Recent change
The average of the latest observations compared with the previous equally sized group. Each group uses up to 30 observations. This is not simply yesterday’s value versus today’s.
Acceleration
How much the recent growth rate changed from the previous rate, in percentage points. Positive acceleration means the rate increased; values can still be falling.
Volatility
How widely values vary relative to their average across this history. Higher volatility means larger fluctuations; it does not measure the chance of future growth.

How the numbers are made

Known limits

  • Topics are matched by name. “Apple” the company and the fruit can be mixed up; the Wikipedia article chosen is shown on every topic page.
  • A Wikipedia language edition is only a rough stand-in for a country. United States, United Kingdom and Worldwide all use English Wikipedia.
  • Hacker News is global in every market and leans heavily towards technology.
  • News coverage comes from one newspaper, The Guardian (UK-based), the same in every market. It is not all news media.
  • Google’s RSS list has no history of its own. ScopeFlux only knows what it has recorded since it started storing the list.
  • Discover is rebuilt about once a day per market, in small steps. The time of the last update is shown at the top.

Why there is no Google search data

Google does not publish search volume. The official Trends API is limited to approved partners, and scraping the public pages breaks Google’s terms. ScopeFlux uses the public daily trending feed where it can, and measures interest where the data is genuinely open.

How CSV analytics, Compare and Seasonality calculate

Data provenance

ScopeFlux analyzes Wikipedia article views and imported CSV series. The RSS list retrieves Google’s public RSS feed. No historical keyword API is connected, and no generated or sample values are shown anywhere. The source of imported files is supplied by the user and has not been independently verified.

Recent growth & acceleration

Recent change compares the means of two adjacent windows, each containing up to 30 observations. A third window supplies the previous growth rate. Acceleration is their difference in percentage points. A zero baseline returns “Not available”, never infinite growth. A point means one supplied observation, not necessarily one day.

Volatility & anomaly flags

Volatility is standard deviation divided by the full-series mean. Exploratory spike and dip flags use the median of up to 30 previous observations and a threshold of four scaled median absolute deviations, with a 25% baseline floor and one-unit minimum. These flags are descriptive, not statistically calibrated alerts or forecasts.

Seasonality

Monthly averages are calculated for calendar years with observations in all 12 months. At least two such years are required. Each year is normalized to an average of 100 before the corresponding months are averaged. Pattern consistency is the ratio of variation in the monthly profile to total normalized variation; it is not a confidence score or prediction accuracy.

Comparison & missing values

Comparison uses only dates shared by all selected series and rebases each to 100 from its first seven common observations. It compares relative evolution, not absolute popularity. Missing dates are not interpolated. CSV imports reject invalid dates, duplicate dates, missing values, negative values, and censored values such as <1.
